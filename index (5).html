About

I am a materials scientist and electron microscopist with research experience in Japan, Cambridge and Bristol. My work centres on growing graphene, hexagonal boron nitride and transition metal dichalcogenides by chemical vapour deposition, and on following how these materials form and change inside the microscope using in situ and operando TEM.

I now manage the electron microscope suite in the Chemical Imaging Facility at the University of Bristol, where I combine facility work, user training and analytical service with my own experiments on metal catalysed carbon growth.

Research

From synthesis to mechanism.

My work connects the preparation of materials with direct observation of their growth, structural evolution and response to heat or electrical bias.

In situ and operando microscopy

I use heating and biasing holders in the TEM to follow catalytic graphitisation, electromigration and the formation of carbon nanostructures as they happen. Watching the intermediate stages is what connects a processing condition to the final structure.

Metal catalysed carbon growth

I have compared ten metal catalysts (Fe, Ni, Cu, Mo, Sn, In, Ga, Co, Pd and CuNi) for carbon transformation. This work produced graphene growth at 180 °C with tin and 150 °C with indium, the lowest temperatures reported at the time.

Wafer scale 2D materials

At the Cambridge Graphene Centre I led wafer scale CVD growth of graphene, hBN, MoS2 and WS2 up to 200 mm within the EU Graphene Flagship, tuning multi step recipes for uniformity, layer number and device quality, and developing polymer assisted transfer onto arbitrary substrates.

Waste derived carbon

I grow graphene from solid waste plastic, and collaborate on studies using waste chicken fat and other waste or renewable precursors. The question throughout is how precursor and catalyst choice shapes the carbon product.

Capability

What I can run.

Techniques, instruments and processes I work with day to day, in the facility and in my own experiments.

  • Electron
    microscopy
    TEM and STEM, including HRTEM, HAADF, EELS, EDX, SAED, CBED and two beam diffraction contrast imaging. In situ and operando work with heating and biasing holders. SEM and EDX. Specimen preparation for powders, films and cross sections.
  • Growth &
    deposition
    CVD at atmospheric and low pressure, MOCVD, magnetron sputtering and thermal evaporation. Wafer scale growth up to 200 mm. Polymer assisted transfer of 2D films onto other substrates.
  • Characterisation
    Raman spectroscopy, XPS, AFM and XRD, used to link processing conditions to structure and properties.
  • Facility work
    Instrument operation and maintenance, user training and competency sign off, written procedures, chemical inventory and safety compliance, vacuum and high voltage systems, and specification of upgrades with manufacturers.
  • Software
    Python for routine processing of diffraction and imaging datasets, plus tools for SAED and XRD pattern analysis and for cross referencing candidate materials against crystallographic databases and published literature.
